Urban Strife

An isometric, Jagged Alliance-style tactical survival RPG set in a zombie-infested American South. I joined remotely to redesign the game's entire UI, raising the quality of every HUD, menu and widget while keeping the retro, old-school TBS post-apocalyptic identity at the heart of the game.

The challenge

The game already had a working UI, but most of it had to be redone. The brief was a balancing act.

Keep the soul

The audience grew up on classic TBS RPGs. The new UI had to look clearly higher-end while still feeling gritty and old-school.

Information density

Combat shows AP, stance, fire mode, Overwatch, reload state and inventory at once, over a busy isometric battlefield.

Breadth

Combat, inventory, stashes, trader, shelter industry, crafting, recycling, map and front-end all needed one shared visual language.

01

Retro-modern art direction

  • Defined a diegetic, hardware-inspired visual language: worn metal casings, LED indicators, CRT readouts, clipboards, ledgers and field journals. It kept the old-school spirit while raising texture, lighting and material quality.
  • Unified HUDs, menus and widgets under one consistent system, so the game reads as a single cohesive product.
02

Tactical combat HUD

  • Redesigned the combat control panel: stance (crouch/run), reload, inventory, fire-mode selector (single/ranged/melee) and Overwatch/radar toggles, housed in a "ballistic calculator" device.
  • Engineered full state sets for every control (default, hover, active, disabled) and a color-coded LED system (green/yellow/red/off) for at-a-glance feedback.
  • Designed turn and objective readouts, cursors and targeting assets, including the sniper aim cursor and stealth icons.
03

Inventory, stash & trade

  • Rebuilt the inventory UI and produced a standardized item-icon library (weapons, ammo, consumables, crafting materials) on a shared template for consistent scale, angle and lighting.
  • Designed the container screens (personal inventory, Normal Stash, Sheriff Stash, Trader), each with its own material identity, plus tactile hand-cursor states for grab, take-all and trade.
04

Shelter, crafting & base management

  • Spearheaded the shelter HUD as a field journal with tabs (Shelter, Team, Map, Diplomacy, To-Do, Notes): census, stockpile & production tables, and a blueprint-style upgrade plan with locked states.
  • Designed the Industry build orders, Warehouse ledger, Shelter Dump and crafting assembly/disassembly screens, so the scavenge → craft → upgrade loop reads like real shelter paperwork.
05

World map

  • Designed the Urban County map as a vintage transportation map, with location markers, travel and scavenge modes, locked areas and random-encounter notes clipped to the side.
06

Front-end, intro & loading

  • Designed the main-menu buttons, comic-panel intro screens and loading screens built around photographs and 3D-rendered props (notebook, M1911, ammunition).
  • Created the tutorial pop-up widget used to walk new players through movement and combat.

Key takeaways

Retro is a design problem

Modernizing a retro identity isn't a polish pass. The hard part was deciding what not to change, so the game stayed true to its old-school TBS roots.

State design is half the UI

In a tactical game, hover, disabled and active states carry as much information as the layout itself.

Diegetic needs a system

A gritty, physical look only scales across dozens of screens when it sits on consistent templates and grids.
