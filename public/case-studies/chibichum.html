01 — Project Overview & Product Identity

OVERVIEW & IDENTITY

ChibiChum is the single product identity for a game that was previously referred to as Retro Petz and as Eastern European Tamagotchi. At its core it is a Tamagotchi-style care game: a small creature with needs (hunger, energy, happiness and hygiene), living in a room the player decorates, rendered in 8-bit pixel art.

What sets it apart is where it lives. Rather than asking for a full-screen session, the pet sits on the desktop, in a browser tab or on a phone, and rewards a few seconds of attention at a time.

02 — Why a Desktop Taskbar & Mobile Companion Pet?

WHY A DESKTOP PET?

Product vision & UX value

The appeal of a virtual pet is the small, repeatable satisfaction of looking after something. Many pet games, though, ask for what a busy day rarely has: a dedicated screen and a dedicated session. ChibiChum starts from the opposite end. The pet goes where the person already is, and the experience is built from moments that take seconds.

Unobtrusive companionship

A traditional full-screen game competes with whatever else the player is doing. A taskbar or desktop pet does not. It offers low-friction micro-interactions, such as a click, a pet, or a glance at what it is up to, that brighten up a daily routine without taking it over. The pet roams its room on its own, so it is a presence rather than a task.

Design consequenceEvery interaction has to be quick, optional and forgiving.
Productivity & micro-breaks

Care needs surface as gentle reminders and non-intrusive notification nudges: hunger, care, cleanliness. During a long study or work session they act as small cues to look away from the task for a minute, so they are designed as invitations rather than alarms.

Design consequenceThe wording and frequency of a nudge matter as much as the mechanism that delivers it.
Cross-platform accessibility

The companion goes where the player goes. It docks in the Windows system tray while they work, and is available on the web and on mobile for downtime. The platform changes; the product identity does not.

Design principles
Glanceable

Understandable at a glance, rewarding in seconds.

Gentle

Nudges are invitations, not interruptions.

Cozy

A warm 8-bit look and a relaxed pace.

Everywhere

One identity across desktop, web and mobile.

03 — Key Game Systems & Features Showcase

GAME SYSTEMS & FEATURES

ChibiChum is built from five connected systems. Needs create the reasons to act, care routines and minigames provide the interaction, and the shop and economy give the player something to build toward.

01 / 05

Pet AI & room dynamics

A pet with a life of its own, in a room that needs upkeep too.

  • Autonomous roaming. The pet wanders its room on its own, so the screen is alive without any input.
  • Multi-toy physics and interaction. Several toys share the room and respond to being played with.
  • Room cleaning. The room needs upkeep as well as the pet.
  • State decay loops. Hunger, energy, happiness and hygiene decay in real time and give the player a reason to care.
02 / 05

Interactive care routines

Each routine turns upkeep into a small piece of play instead of a menu action.

  • Petting. An animation with floating hearts gives immediate, readable feedback.
  • Fitness. A dumbbell minigame.
  • Grooming. Teeth brushing and bathtub care.
03 / 05

Fruit Slash minigame

A quick, skill-based change of pace from the care loop.

  • A fruit-ninja-style mode in which the player swipes or slashes fruit that spawns dynamically.
  • It fits the product’s micro-break philosophy.
04 / 05

Room customization & shop

Customization gives the pet a place that feels like the player’s own.

  • Independent wallpaper slots and flooring customization let players compose their own room.
  • Seasonal decor: a Christmas tree, plus Easter and Halloween holiday themes.
  • 8-bit coloring sketchbook with buyable crayon packs.
  • Seasonal items give the room a reason to change through the year.
05 / 05

Monetization architecture

Free-to-play, with a GP currency and a rewarded-ad fallback.

  • ChibiChum is free-to-play and uses a GP currency.
  • A rewarded commercial/ad fallback system, coordinated through AdManager, handles the rewarded-ad side of the economy.
  • Keeping ad handling behind a single manager keeps monetization logic out of the gameplay code.
04 — Technical Architecture & Multi-Platform Delivery

ARCHITECTURE & DELIVERY

The goal was to ship the same game on Windows, the web and mobile without rewriting its core logic for each. The approach is to keep everything that makes ChibiChum a game on one side of a boundary, and everything that makes it an app on a particular platform on the other.

Conceptual layering, simplified.

Core engine separation

The game loop, the state managers and sprite animation rendering are modular and independent of any platform wrapper. A wrapper hosts the engine and provides what the operating system needs. It does not contain gameplay.

Windows desktop companion mode

A frameless, transparent window overlay with no window chrome, so the game appears to sit directly on the desktop. System tray minimization tucks the pet away without quitting the game, and care nudges arrive through the operating system’s own native notifications.

Web & mobile packaging

Web. An optimized canvas export targets web portals such as Itch.io.

06 — Project Key Takeaways & Lessons Learned

KEY TAKEAWAYS

What the project taught, in two groups: engineering lessons from building it, and UX and product lessons about how a companion should behave.

6.1

Engineering

1
Pixel-art panels need slice-aware scaling

UI panels cut from sprite sheets stay crisp only when the corners keep their exact pixels and the edges and center do the stretching. Using the wrong resize mode on panel images distorted the art in the original mobile build. Treating panels as 9-slice assets lets them scale cleanly at any size.

2
One source of truth for sizing

Hard-coded dimensions drift as screens multiply. The original mobile build centralized device-dependent sizing in a single useDeviceMetrics hook, so screens share one set of sizing rules.

3
Treat animation as a state machine

Modeling the pet’s behavior as explicit states with defined transitions, and blending animations between them, lets autonomous roaming and player-triggered reactions such as petting coexist without fighting each other.

4
An always-on window is judged on its idle cost

A companion that stays open all day beside real work earns its place by being cheap to keep running, not by its peak frame rate. Keeping the window lightweight is what makes it welcome rather than merely tolerated.

5
Keep platform code at the edges

With the loop, state and rendering in a shared core, wrappers stay thin and gameplay changes do not need per-platform rewrites.

6.2

UX & product

6
Nudges are invitations, not interruptions

Notifications work as small cues for a break. Their tone and frequency are part of the design, not an afterthought.

7
Personalization creates ownership

A room the player has decorated, with seasonal items and a sketchbook to color in, turns a generic pet into their pet.

8
Monetize around the cozy loop, not through it

Rewarded ads and a free-to-play economy fit a cozy game only when they stay out of the care loop. Isolating them behind AdManager keeps that boundary explicit.

Outcome

A FAMILIAR IDEA, FITTED TO A MODERN DAY

The virtual pet is a familiar idea. ChibiChum fits it to a modern day: small enough to live in a taskbar, rich enough to carry a care loop, minigames, a shop and a seasonal room, and built to reach three platforms from one core.
