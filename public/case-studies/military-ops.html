UI/UX Design · Ghost Recon (Unlaunched)

MILITARY OPS

A full UI/UX system built for an unlaunched, Ghost Recon–branded military live-service shooter — home & landing, player identity, post-match debrief, weekly progression, a full player-reporting & moderation system, the underlying UI kit, and an animated tactical-tablet interface.

Project Status: Unlaunched / Prototype
01 — Overview

WHAT THIS PROJECT WAS

This was UI/UX design work for an unlaunched military live-service title referencing the Ghost Recon brand internally. The scope covered the full player journey: the home/landing HUD, player identity (specialization cards), post-match debrief screens, a weekly assignments & progression system, and a complete player-reporting and moderation flow — all built on a shared UI kit (colors, buttons, cursor).

The project did not ship. The pages below walk through the systems as designed.

02 — Home & Landing

HOME & LANDING

The landing screen surfaces the current squad, an assignment tracker (capped at two lines per entry — deliberately no scroll, to keep the panel from breaking), promotional tiles, operator switching, and the current match mode — all readable at a glance before a match starts.

03 — Player Identity

PLAYER IDENTITY

Player cards carry a specialization (Demolition Expert, Sniper, Ghost Recon), callsign, rank, and platform identity — designed to read clearly whether shown solo or side-by-side with squadmates.

04 — Post-Match Debrief

POST-MATCH DEBRIEF

Two directions for the end-of-match summary: one breaking the score down by category (combat, economy, objective) against a rolling history of the last 10 matches, and a second exploring a playstyle read-out on a triangular chart, labeling the player's session (e.g. “Casualty”) from the same three axes.

05 — Weekly Assignments & Progression

WEEKLY PROGRESSION

Players choose between two weekly objective packages (each built from the same underlying pool of objectives, reframed under a different theme), then track progress against a week-long checklist with objectives unlocking in sequence and their locations pinned on the map.

06 — Reporting a Player

REPORTING A PLAYER

The report flow needed two contexts — from the menus, and without interrupting play in-game — with different category sets for each (general conduct in menus; conduct and cheating call-outs in-game, since that's where cheating is actually witnessed). Each went through a wireframe pass before the visual design.

CONSEQUENCES & FEEDBACK

08 — UI Kit & Visual System

UI KIT & VISUAL SYSTEM

The shared foundation underneath every screen above: a custom cursor and reticle-style icon set, a four-category color system (Brand, Extended, Rarity, Party) with tonal variants, and a full button-state matrix (Default, CTA, Blocked, Disabled, Error × Normal, Hover, Click).

09 — TAC-OS: The Tactical Tablet

TAC-OS TABLET SYSTEM

TAC-OS is the in-fiction operating system running on the player's tactical tablet — built as its own consistent design language so every tablet screen feels like the same device, no matter which tool is open, and sharing its color scheme with the main game UI.

The drone-hacking sequence was specced as a 5-second, three-state animation — a fixed format (4:3, 640×480, WebM VP9, no audio) so it could be produced consistently in After Effects and Blender.

10 — Outcome

UNLAUNCHED, NOT UNFINISHED

This project didn't ship — but the systems above were carried from wireframe to finished mockup across every major player-facing surface: onboarding into a match, identity, progression, moderation, and the tactical tablet's own motion language. It's a fuller picture of end-to-end UI/UX ownership than a single shipped feature usually shows.
